<SEE-and-SAW> - 2025, Studio white clay, glaze, screw, plywood
By @hyeyoonlillyjung

This piece explores the illusion of freedom and the hidden mechanisms of self-imposed confinement. Manually operated, it depicts birds seemingly playful on a seesaw yet their bodies are trapped within. It symbolizes how people often become addicted to stimulation while believing they are simply “having fun.” Through this imagery, I aim to question how modern life disguises obsession as play, and how we unconsciously trap ourselves within systems we create.

The seesaw represents the tension between balance and captivity, while the removable blue birds serve as addicted ones. The one watching represents the one who managed to break out of that addiction and observes the ones left behind with pity. The act of removing the birds becomes a metaphor for freeing oneself or others from cycles of addiction and judgment. This piece conceptually reflects my personal introspection; in judging others, I find myself confined within my own standards and expectations.
